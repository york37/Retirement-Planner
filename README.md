# Northstar Canadian Retirement Planner

A dependency-free browser application for Canadian retirement scenarios. **Open `Northstar.html` directly in a modern browser**; no installation or server is required. It includes the same app as the hosted/static version. To regenerate it after source changes, run `node build-standalone.mjs`.

For development, run `node server.mjs` and open http://127.0.0.1:5173. Run `node --test tests/*.test.mjs` for financial-model verification. Deploy the `dist` folder to any static host. Private Sites publishing is pending explicit user approval after automatic approval review blocked source synchronization; no version has been uploaded or deployed.

Tax residency is fixed to Ontario, Canada. Inputs cover ages, TFSA/RRSP/non-registered balances, contributions, asset allocations, spending, inflation, pension income/start age, CPP/OAS amounts, other taxable income context and editable nominal returns. Inputs are automatically saved to browser local storage and restored on reload in the same browser/profile and file location. Cookies are not used, so nothing is attached to HTTP requests. No financial data is transmitted; external font loads have been removed, and a content security policy blocks network connections and form submissions. Reset clears this app’s saved inputs and restores defaults. Browser storage restrictions or clearing local site data may prevent restoration. The adjusted-cost-basis input has been removed; the initial non-registered cost basis is assumed to equal its entered balance, ignoring pre-existing unrealized gains/losses. The annual table and CSV expose all calculations. Methodology and primary source links are included in the app.

The baseline uses 2026 tax brackets and constant-return projections. Income-tax estimates include the credits and adjustments documented in the app; this is not a complete tax-return or benefits engine. Non-registered investments are modeled as global/foreign equity distributions plus capital appreciation and bond interest. Canadian dividend tax credits and foreign tax credits are outside scope. Pension/benefit payments fund their attributable taxes. Before retirement, investment-income tax is funded externally, preserving the entered net savings and preventing discretionary withdrawals. During retirement, investment taxes use the non-registered account first and then the remaining priority order. Living-cost withdrawals follow the selected priority and include the progressive income-tax top-up; other-income context taxes remain external. Unfunded spending/tax is shown explicitly. Savings require sufficient personal contribution room.

The age entered is the modeled age at January 1, 2026. At the beginning of each modeled year, pension/benefit income and mandatory RRIF payments fund their initial taxes and retirement expenses; investments cover any remaining need. Surplus income is invested in non-registered assets, growth occurs next, remaining taxes are paid, and savings are added at year-end before retirement. Final planning year is inclusive. RRSP converts at end of age 71; age-72 RRIF minimums start the following year. Excess distributions are reinvested in non-registered assets. Real end-of-year amounts are deflated by `(1 + inflation) ** (year - 2026 + 1)`.

Historical defaults are 1900–2019 world real geometric returns (5.2% equity, 2.0% bonds) from the 2020 Yearbook/London Business School, converted to default nominal returns using 2% inflation. Inflation defaults to 2%, equity return to 7.304%, and bond return to 4.04%. Each field is editable immediately; changing inflation does not overwrite nominal return assumptions. They are not claimed to be historical CAD nominal returns or forecasts. The 2026 UBS release was researched but its headline US series is not used as a global proxy.

The 2026 federal and Ontario brackets, basic/age/pension credit amounts, Ontario surtax thresholds ($5,818/$7,446), basic reduction and health premium were rechecked against CRA and Ontario primary sources on October 18, 2026. The tax graph has two tabs from retirement through the final planning age: Effective tax rate (the default percentage view) and Tax payable amounts (nominal and inflation-adjusted dollars). Its tooltip shows the effective rate, taxable income, federal/Ontario breakdown and OAS recovery; the rate is total modeled payable divided by modeled taxable income. The annual table and CSV show taxable income, federal tax, Ontario tax, OAS recovery, combined tax payable and effective rate for each year. Tax on other-income context is included in that rate, while only incremental pension/benefit and portfolio taxes are funded by those payments and accounts. A dash (blank CSV cell) means zero taxable income; CSV rates are in percentage points. Future threshold/credit indexation remains a projection assumption rather than a claim about future legislation.

The tax audit also corrected annual tax reporting when carried-forward capital losses are consumed: the year's tax is now captured before updating the loss balance for the next year.

Default current age is 46 and the final planning age is 90. Existing saved inputs continue to take precedence until Reset is used. All dollar inputs display thousands separators, accept manually entered amounts, and retain $1,000 increment/decrement controls and arrow-key stepping. Font sizes are increased by 25% throughout the interface and chart labels.

Withdrawal source reporting uses actual per-account gross sales/payments, including tax-funded withdrawals and mandatory RRIF payments. RRSP/RRIF is purple, non-registered is amber, and TFSA is teal. Three aligned graph tracks show overlapping withdrawal periods. The annual table and CSV include each account's year-end balance, gross withdrawals, and any RRIF surplus reinvested in non-registered assets. Priority applies to discretionary living-cost spending, with mandatory RRIF payments first. Year-end investment taxes use non-registered assets first, and working-year investment taxes are paid externally. Per-account growth and contribution ledgers support balance-reconciliation tests.

The projection shows one set of actual year-end balances in nominal and inflation-adjusted dollars. The annual table and CSV place effective tax rate and tax payable (federal plus Ontario income tax and any OAS recovery) immediately after year and age. Investment growth and actual portfolio taxes are listed separately.

Pension and CPP/OAS inputs are annual amounts in today’s dollars, defaulting to $0. Pension defaults to start age 65, with a user-selected whole-number start age; CPP and OAS start at 65. All three are indexed from the current modeled year using the inflation assumption through the inclusive final planning year. Income received before retirement is invested, in addition to scheduled savings; after retirement it covers expenses first. Surplus income is reinvested in non-registered assets and reported separately from RRIF surplus. The pension is treated as an eligible lifetime employer pension; CPP and OAS are taxable but do not earn pension income credits. OAS recovery is 15% of income over $95,323 (2026), capped at gross OAS received, deducted from taxable income, and added to payable. Its threshold follows the tax-indexing option. The model uses annual settlement rather than next-July withholding, and constant purchasing power rather than an automatic age-75 OAS step-up. Pension/CPP/OAS amounts and recovery appear in the annual table/CSV; payments also appear in the graph tooltip. Inputs retain comma formatting, $1,000 controls, local persistence and Reset support.

Both time-series graphs begin at the entered retirement age and use age labels at retirement, retirement + 5, retirement + 10, and so on, with calendar years below. They still plot every annual value, including an off-cycle final planning age. The portfolio withdrawal tracks use the same retirement range. The annual table/CSV retain every modeled year, including working years. Narrow charts scroll horizontally to preserve all five-year labels.

The tax graph tabs support click/tap and Left/Right/Home/End keyboard navigation. Both tabs use the same yearly calculations and five-year retirement timeline. Effective-rate years without taxable income appear as gaps; an actual zero rate remains plotted as 0%. The table/CSV retain both tax amounts and effective rates.

The withdrawal audit separates RRSP and RRIF year-end balances and cash withdrawals. Conversion at the end of age 71 is a non-cash internal transfer, with no tax or minimum in that year. Mandatory RRIF payments start at 72, even if retirement is later; those statutory payments are the only pre-retirement withdrawal exception. The table reports registered cash used for living, its income-tax top-up, any investment-tax draw and the mandatory minimum, which reconcile to gross registered cash withdrawals. With default RRSP-first inputs, age-65 spending is $69,926.94 (today’s $48,000 inflated for 19 years), and the registered draw is $79,717.63 = $69,926.94 living cash + $9,790.70 income tax. Tax on non-registered growth is funded separately from that account, rather than increasing the RRSP draw. Independent bracket-formula tests and annual account/cash-flow reconciliation cover this scenario.

A tax-basis readout below the tax graph displays taxable income × effective rate ≈ annual tax payable for the first retirement year or the year explored in either graph. It lists living expenses, gross portfolio withdrawals and pension/CPP/OAS receipts alongside the equation. The annual table now places taxable income beside the effective rate to clarify the denominator. In an RRSP-only 2026 Ontario example at age 65, $291,226.20 gross less $111,226.20 tax leaves $180,000 for living costs (38.1924% effective). At age 72 with RRIF pension credits, gross is $290,319.20 and tax $110,319.20 (37.9993% effective). These examples were verified using independent top-bracket formulas; the progressive tax and withdrawal solver were retained. Applying the rate to the spending target instead of gross taxable income would underfund the tax payment.
